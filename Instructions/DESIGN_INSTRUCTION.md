Throughout the project, do not generate generic AI-style interfaces. Prioritize intentional design, hierarchy, and consistency over rapid assembly of common UI patterns.

Layout
Do not stack unrelated cards simply to fill space.
Do not center every section by default.
Avoid excessive use of card-based layouts.
Avoid inconsistent spacing between sections.
Do not create layouts that feel like templates.
Every page should have a clear visual structure and purpose.
Use grid systems instead of randomly placed components.
Leave generous negative space.
Colors
Avoid random accent colors.
Do not assign a different color to every widget.
Never rely on gradients as the primary visual element.
Avoid bright "startup" color palettes.
Maintain a restrained monochrome foundation with one accent color.
Colors should communicate meaning, not decoration.
Typography
Do not make every heading the same size.
Avoid weak visual hierarchy.
Do not use oversized body text.
Avoid excessive font weights.
Pair large editorial headings with small supporting text.
Use monospaced typography only for metadata and technical information.
Components
Avoid rounded rectangles everywhere.
Avoid excessive shadows.
Avoid glassmorphism unless there is a functional reason.
Avoid neumorphism.
Avoid oversized buttons.
Avoid unnecessary borders.
Components should feel engineered rather than decorative.
Icons
Do not mix icon libraries.
Avoid colorful icons.
Avoid filled icons unless necessary.
Prefer thin stroke icons with consistent weight.
Icons should support information, not dominate it.
Navigation
Avoid oversized sidebars.
Avoid hamburger menus on desktop.
Do not make navigation resemble a mobile application.
Navigation should be simple, minimal, and information-dense.
Cards
Do not place every piece of information inside a separate card.
Avoid dashboards consisting entirely of floating boxes.
Prefer sections, grids, dividers, and typography to separate content.
Cards should only exist when they improve grouping.
Forms
Avoid extremely rounded inputs.
Avoid oversized labels.
Avoid excessive helper text.
Inputs should align cleanly on a grid.
Keep forms compact and readable.
Buttons
Avoid giant CTA buttons.
Do not use multiple primary buttons in one viewport.
Limit the number of button styles.
Buttons should have a clear hierarchy.
Tables
Prefer tables over cards for structured datasets.
Avoid converting tabular information into repeated cards.
Preserve density while maintaining readability.
Make sorting and filtering obvious.
Charts
Avoid rainbow color palettes.
Avoid unnecessary 3D effects.
Avoid excessive animations.
Use restrained colors and minimal gridlines.
Charts should emphasize the data, not the visualization.
Motion
Avoid unnecessary animations.
Avoid bounce effects.
Avoid elastic transitions.
Avoid parallax scrolling.
Use subtle opacity and position transitions only when they improve usability.
Visual Effects
Avoid glowing borders.
Avoid neon effects.
Avoid heavy blur.
Avoid background particle animations.
Avoid decorative noise that does not communicate information.
Spacing
Avoid cramped layouts.
Avoid inconsistent padding.
Use a spacing system (4px or 8px scale).
Maintain consistent vertical rhythm.
Branding
Avoid generic startup illustrations.
Avoid stock-looking hero sections.
Avoid meaningless marketing copy.
Prefer strong typography over decorative graphics.
Information Architecture
Every screen must communicate a primary purpose within 3 seconds.
Group related functionality logically.
Reduce cognitive load.
Minimize unnecessary clicks.
Never hide important actions behind multiple layers of navigation.
General Principles
Design every screen intentionally rather than assembling reusable components.
Favor clarity over decoration.
Favor typography over graphics.
Favor grids over floating layouts.
Favor whitespace over separators.
Favor consistency over novelty.
Every visual element must have a functional purpose.
The interface should feel designed by a product designer, not generated by an AI.
