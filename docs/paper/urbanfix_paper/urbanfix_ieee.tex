\begin{abstract}
Most digital grievance systems classify complaint text and track tickets. They rarely check the photo a citizen attaches, their urgency estimates cannot be traced to inputs, and only the administration sees the records. We present UrbanFix, an urban governance platform that follows a complaint from the citizen's photo to a verified, publicly recorded repair and on to research data. Citizens file without an account using an email code. A YOLOv8 model trained for the chosen category localises the defect in each photo, and a weighted questionnaire gives an urgency score anyone can recompute. A seven-stage workflow enforced on the server requires ward-level assignment, photo proof and a receipt before closure. Every complaint is published without personal data, and approved researchers get logged, time-limited access to an anonymised projection. Validation mAP50 is 0.724 for potholes and 0.502 for litter (masks), and 0.908 for open manholes and 0.729 for graffiti (boxes), at 19 to 33 ms per image on a laptop CPU. When the model is unsure, it flags the report for a human reviewer.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Recent systems route complaint text \cite{citiwatch,smartcity}, predict urgency \cite{cholke}, classify waste images \cite{regenx} or filter unsafe photos \cite{civicmate}. None checks whether a photo shows the reported defect, their urgency cannot be traced to an input, and their records stay with officials or the filer. UrbanFix runs each complaint through six steps in one system (Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}). Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item A YOLOv8 check \cite{yolov8} for each category, offered only where a public dataset with localised labels exists.
\item Prioritisation that puts the questionnaire score and the model's evidence in front of a supervisor, who decides; the model cannot reject a report.
\item A workflow modelled as a guarded state machine with ward-level assignment, photo proof and an append-only history.
\item A public registry with identity removed on the server, and a research gateway with scoped, expiring and logged access to pseudonymised records.
\end{enumerate}

\section{System Architecture}\label{sec:arch}
One Expo client serves all six roles, and the server decides which screens a user sees (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). Each request passes an Express pipeline that re-checks the account, any pending invitation and research expiry on every call. Intake sends photos to a separate FastAPI service only after returning the tracking ID, so a slow or failed model call cannot affect the filing. Each MongoDB complaint document embeds its status history and AI analysis.

\section{Methodology}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Category selection by dataset availability}
Let $\mathcal{K}$ be the candidate complaint categories. A category $k$ is offered only if a public dataset $\mathcal{D}_k$ of street-level images with localised labels (boxes or masks), documented provenance and a usable licence exists:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}=\{\,k\in\mathcal{K} : \mathcal{D}_k \text{ is public} \wedge \text{localised} \wedge \text{street-level}\,\}.
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
Four of ten candidate categories qualify (Fig.~\ref{fig:gate}), and the API rejects the rest.

\subsection{Traceable questionnaire urgency}
Each category has five yes/no questions $q_i$ with weights $w_i\in\{1,2\}$ (2 for safety-critical). With answers $y_i\in\{0,1\}$, the urgency score and band are
\begin{equation}
U=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i y_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{5} w_i},\qquad
u_Q=\begin{cases}\text{High}, & U\ge 0.6\\ \text{Medium}, & 0.3\le U<0.6\\ \text{Standard}, & U<0.3.\end{cases}
\label{eq:urg}
\end{equation}
Dividing by $\sum w_i$ keeps categories with different weights comparable. The citizen sees $u_Q$ while filing, and anyone can recompute it from the stored answers (Fig.~\ref{fig:urg}).

\subsection{Per-category vision validation}\label{sec:ai}
For a complaint of category $k$ with photos $x_1,\dots,x_n$ ($n\le3$), the model $f_k$ returns detections $\mathcal{B}_m=f_k(x_m)=\{(b_j,s_j,M_j)\}$ with boxes $b_j$, confidences $s_j$ and, for segmentation models, masks $M_j$. The service keeps detections with $s_j\ge\tau$ ($\tau=0.25$) after non-maximum suppression and computes
\begin{align}
s^{*}&=\max_{m}\max_{j} s_j, \label{eq:smax}\\
\delta&=\max_{m}\frac{\bigl|\bigcup_j M_j^{(m)}\bigr|}{H_m W_m}, \label{eq:delta}\\
r&=\mathbf{1}\left[s^{*}<\tau\right], \label{eq:review}
\end{align}
where $\delta$ is the masked share of the image and $r$ is the \texttt{needsReview} flag. The suggested urgency $u_{AI}$ is $u_Q$ raised one band when $\delta>\theta_\delta$. The model never changes a complaint's stage; the supervisor decides with all of this in view (Fig.~\ref{fig:aipipe}).

\subsection{Accountable workflow}\label{sec:wf}
The workflow is a deterministic finite-state machine $\mathcal{M}=(S,A,\Delta,s_0,F)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:fsm}) with
\begin{align*}
S&=\{\textsf{sub},\textsf{acc},\textsf{asg},\textsf{wip},\textsf{prf},\textsf{cls},\textsf{rej}\},\\
A&=\{\textit{accept},\textit{reject},\textit{assign},\textit{start},\textit{proof},\textit{close},\textit{rework}\},
\end{align*}
start $s_0=\textsf{sub}$ and terminal set $F=\{\textsf{cls},\textsf{rej}\}$. A transition is applied only if its guard holds:
\begin{equation}
\Delta(s,a)\ \text{is defined} \iff s\in\mathcal{S}_a \wedge \rho(\text{actor})\in\mathcal{R}_a \wedge g_a(c),
\label{eq:fsm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{S}_a$ and $\mathcal{R}_a$ are the allowed source stages and roles, and the guard $g_a$ checks remarks ($\ge$10 characters), the ward and leave status of the assignee, and the proof photos. Each transition appends $(\text{stage},\text{actor},\text{remarks},t)$ to the history, and \textit{close} emails a PDF receipt. The public sees $\varphi(s)\in\{$Pending, In Progress, Resolved, Rejected$\}$.

\subsection{Tiered disclosure and research projection}\label{sec:priv}
Each audience gets a fixed server-side projection of the same record (Table~\ref{tab:disc}). Researchers get eight fields
\begin{equation}
\pi(c)=\bigl(h_\kappa(c.\mathit{id}),\,k,\,u_Q,\,\varphi,\,\mathit{ward},\,\lfloor t_f\rfloor_{d},\,\lfloor t_c\rfloor_{d},\,\Delta t\bigr),
\label{eq:proj}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h_\kappa(x)=\mathrm{trunc}_{48}\bigl(\mathrm{HMAC\text{-}SHA256}(\kappa,x)\bigr),\quad \Delta t=\tfrac{t_c-t_f}{86400\,\mathrm{s}},
\label{eq:hmac}
\end{equation}
where $\lfloor\cdot\rfloor_d$ truncates to the day and $\kappa$ is a server secret, so IDs join across exports but cannot be reversed. A request $q$ under scope $\sigma$ and expiry $t_e\le t_g+180$ days is served only if
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
&t_q<t_e\ \wedge\ \bigl(\text{rows}(q)\Rightarrow\sigma=\text{records}\bigr)\\
&\wedge\ n_{\mathrm{exp}}(d)\le5\ \wedge\ |q|\le5000,
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:quota}
\end{equation}
Fig.~\ref{fig:access} shows the full chain.

\section{Implementation and Experimental Setup}\label{sec:impl}
The stack is Expo~57, Node.js~20 with Express and Mongoose, and FastAPI with Ultralytics~8.4 \cite{yolov8}. Each model is fine-tuned from COCO YOLOv8n weights at $640\times640$, batch 16, up to 60 epochs with patience 15, on an Apple M5 laptop (Table~\ref{tab:data}, Fig.~\ref{fig:train}); training took 29 to 52 minutes.

We report precision $P=\frac{TP}{TP+FP}$, recall $R=\frac{TP}{TP+FN}$ and
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AP}=\int_{0}^{1} p(r)\,dr,
\label{eq:ap}
\end{equation}
with mAP50 at IoU 0.5 and mAP50--95 averaged over 0.50 to 0.95.

\subsection{Limitations}
Common complaints such as water leakage are unsupported for lack of data, and the models have not been tested on Indian street photos. We set the questionnaire weights and $\theta_\delta$ by hand. There is no GPS or duplicate detection, and the workflow numbers come from seeded data.

% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion and Future Work}\label{sec:conc}
UrbanFix takes a civic complaint from the citizen's photo to a recorded repair using open-source software and public data. A per-category model checks the photo, a supervisor sets the priority, and the server enforces each step before the record is published without personal data. Next we will fine-tune on local images, fit the weights to closed complaints, add GPS and duplicate detection, and run a ward-level pilot with a municipal corporation.
